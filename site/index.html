Why I built this

I wanted to build an agent that shows its working.

I work in pharmaceutical technology in the UK. Most AI demos I see are chatbots that sound sure of themselves and cannot say how they got to an answer. In a regulated setting that is the whole problem, so I built the opposite: a small agent whose every step is written down and can be checked afterwards.

RefundRx is a portfolio project, not a product. It runs on synthetic data, and it is built to production patterns: versioned policy, tested scenarios, and an audit trail you can verify.

The problem I saw

Pharmacies do not refund medicines. One refund does exist.

Once a medicine has been dispensed in the UK it cannot go back on the shelf, so there is no general pharmacy refund. The refund that does exist is the NHSBSA prescription-charge refund, claimed on form FP57, for people who paid a charge they were exempt from.

Each claim turns on hard rules: an FP57 receipt issued at the point of payment, a claim made within three months less a day, a valid exemption category, and one refund per form. A model that guesses is the wrong tool. A model that has to show its plan and its checks is a better one.

What it does

A planner, a gatherer and a decision-maker.

  1. 01

    Planner

    A language model writes an ordered plan, but it can only choose from six named checks. A plan that leaves out the receipt check is recorded as a weak plan, not quietly corrected.

  2. 02

    Gather

    Runs each step in the plan and writes every tool call, its arguments and its result to a trace. Retrieving the relevant policy text is one of those logged steps.

  3. 03

    Decision

    Applies the policy rules stored in the database and enforces the receipt rule whatever the plan said. It writes a hash-chained audit row, and on approval records the claim in the same transaction.

How I bridged the gap

Policy lives in the database, and the log can be checked.

The rules are rows in Postgres, not sentences in a prompt, so they can be versioned and tested. Each decision is stored with its plan, its trace and the hash of the row before it. A verifier walks the chain and recomputes every hash.

Evidence

Ten scenarios, each with an expected outcome.

Every scenario has an expected decision and an expected tool trace. The suite runs in CI on SQLite and Postgres with an offline planner. Scoring by a language-model judge runs separately, and I will publish those results once I have a run I can stand behind.

Limits

What this is not.

It is a demonstration on synthetic data. It is not validated for clinical, regulatory or GxP use, and it is not insured for real decisions. It covers exemption-based refunds only: prepayment-certificate and HC3 refunds are out of scope.

A hash chain stored in the same database resists casual edits, not someone who can rewrite every row. Real tamper-evidence would need the chain head anchored somewhere else, which a free-tier demo does not do.
