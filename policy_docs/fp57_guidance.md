## FP57 refund receipt — what it is
An FP57 is an NHS prescription charge refund receipt. If a patient in England pays a prescription charge but may be entitled to free prescriptions, they can ask the pharmacy for an FP57 when they pay. The pharmacy completes it at the time of payment, and it is the document used to claim the refund later.

## FP57 must be obtained at the time of payment
The FP57 must be requested and issued when the charge is paid; a pharmacy cannot issue one afterwards. NHSBSA will consider a refund without an FP57 only in exceptional circumstances, by direct application to NHSBSA — "the pharmacy did not tell me" or "I did not realise I required one" are not accepted reasons. System rule: that exceptional route is outside this system, so a claim with no FP57 issued at payment is refused.

## Time limit for claiming a refund
How long you have to claim: NHSBSA states the refund claim needs to be made within 3 months less a day from the date the prescription charge was paid, and claims made after this time will not be accepted. For example, a charge paid on 1 January must be claimed by 31 March. Late claims can only be raised with NHSBSA Help with Health Costs, which this system does not handle.

## Exemption categories that can support a refund
A refund is available where the patient was entitled to free prescriptions on the date the charge was paid. In-scope categories for this system are: age (60 or over, under 16, or 16 to 18 and in full-time education); a valid maternity exemption certificate (pregnant, or had a baby in the previous 12 months); a valid medical exemption certificate for a specified medical condition; and an HC2 certificate under the NHS Low Income Scheme (full help with health costs).

## Evidence of entitlement
The patient claims at a community pharmacy in England by presenting the FP57 together with evidence of entitlement, such as the exemption certificate, which must cover the date the charge was paid. The pharmacy checks the evidence belongs to the patient and records on the form what it saw. A claim whose exemption category cannot be matched to a recognised entitlement is refused.

## One refund per FP57
System rule: each FP57 supports one refund. The pharmacy records the refund on the form and declares it to NHSBSA, so a second claim on an FP57 that has already been refunded is treated as a duplicate and refused, whichever pharmacy it is presented at.

## Refund amount
The refund is the prescription charge that was actually paid, per item. The NHS prescription charge in England is £9.90 per item, frozen for 2026/27. A refund cannot be made for a charge that was never actually paid.

## Out of scope for this system
Prepayment certificate (PPC) refunds and refunds involving an HC3 certificate (partial help with health costs) follow separate rules and are not decided by this system. Claims of those kinds are declined as out of scope rather than assessed.

## Pharmacies do not refund dispensed medicines
Once a medicine has been dispensed it cannot be returned to stock or resold. Pharmacies accept unwanted medicines for safe disposal only, and controlled drugs require witnessed destruction. The only refund route for a prescription charge is the FP57 process above.
